Palestine

Introduction

Palestine is a historic land in the eastern Mediterranean, known for its ancient cities, religious heritage, cultural traditions and the long struggle of its people for self-determination. Its history stretches back thousands of years and includes many civilizations, empires and communities. Today, Palestine is associated primarily with the Gaza Strip, the West Bank and East Jerusalem. Palestinians have a distinct national identity shaped by their language, traditions, family histories and connection to their homeland. Palestine is also at the center of one of the world's longest-running political conflicts, involving questions of land, displacement, occupation, security, independence and human rights.

The Ottoman Empire governed Palestine for approximately four centuries, until the First World War. Most people lived in villages and towns, working in agriculture, crafts, trade and other occupations. Olive cultivation, grain farming and regional commerce were important parts of the economy. Jerusalem, Hebron, Nablus, Jaffa and other cities developed distinct commercial and cultural traditions. British Rule and the Balfour Declaration

After the First World War, Britain administered Palestine under a League of Nations Mandate. In 1917, Britain issued the Balfour Declaration, expressing support for the establishment of a national home for the Jewish people in Palestine while stating that the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities should not be prejudiced. Jewish immigration increased during the following decades, especially as antisemitism and persecution intensified in Europe. At the same time, Palestinian Arabs sought independence and opposed political developments they believed threatened their rights and future in their homeland. Tensions increased between Jewish and Arab communities, leading to protests, violence and growing political divisions.

The United Nation Partition Plan OF 1947

In 1947, the United Nations proposed dividing British-ruled Palestine into separate Jewish and Arab states, with a special international arrangement for Jerusalem. The Jewish leadership accepted the proposal, while Palestinian Arab leaders and Arab states rejected it, objecting to the proposed division and its terms. Violence increased following the partition vote. Britain ended its Mandate in May 1948, and Israel declared independence. War followed between Israel and neighboring Arab states. The events of 1947 and 1948 permanently transformed the region.

The Nakba of 1948

In June 1967, a war involving Israel and neighboring Arab states changed the political geography of the region. Israel captured the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and the Gaza Strip, along with other territories outside Palestine. The West Bank and Gaza became central to Palestinian demands for independence. Israeli military occupation, settlement expansion, restrictions on movement and disputes over land have remained major sources of tension. The United Nations and the International Court of Justice have addressed the legal consequences of the occupation. Israeli settlements in occupied Palestinian territory are considered illegal under the prevailing international legal position, a position disputed by Israel.

The First and second Intfadas

The First Intifada began in 1987 as a Palestinian uprising against Israeli occupation. It involved demonstrations, strikes, civil disobedience and confrontations with Israeli forces. The Oslo Accords of the 1990s established mutual recognition between Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization and created a framework for limited Palestinian self-government. However, the agreements did not resolve the central disputes over borders, Jerusalem, refugees and settlements. The Second Intifada began in 2000 following failed negotiations and rising tensions. It involved Palestinian attacks, including suicide bombings, and extensive Israeli military operations. Civilians on both sides suffered deaths, injuries and trauma. The violence deepened mistrust and made a negotiated settlement more difficult.

Gaza and the West Bank

Palestine's two principal territories, Gaza and the West Bank, face different but interconnected challenges. The Gaza Strip is a densely populated coastal territory. Hamas won the 2006 Palestinian legislative elections and took control of Gaza in 2007 after violent clashes with Fatah. Israel imposed extensive restrictions on Gaza, while Egypt also restricted movement through its border. Repeated wars, restrictions on trade and movement, and damage to infrastructure severely affected living conditions. The West Bank contains Palestinian cities, towns, villages and Israeli settlements. Palestinians there face military occupation, checkpoints, movement restrictions, settlement expansion and episodes of violence.
